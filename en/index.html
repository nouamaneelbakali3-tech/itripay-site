ConnectionMerchant and customer, one tap apart.

The problem

Your customer has a card. You only take cash.

So the sale walks away. A card machine means a monthly fee and paperwork with the bank. And the paper credit book gets lost, along with the debts in it.

  • The box on the counter

    A payment terminal is a second device to rent, charge and keep track of.

  • The paper carnet

    Who owes what lives in a notebook that can be lost, and nobody adds up the total.

  • The numbers nobody has

    At closing time, the day’s sales are a guess.

How it works

Three steps. One phone.

  1. 1

    Enter the amount

    Type the price on the keypad, the way you would on a calculator.

  2. 2

    Hand over the phone

    The customer sees the amount and taps a card or phone on yours.

  3. 3

    Done

    A receipt, printed or shared on WhatsApp. The sale is already in your records.

Credit carnet

The carnet, without the carnet.

Every neighbourhood shop keeps a notebook of who owes what. ItriPay keeps it for you: each customer, each debt, each payment against it, and a total that is always current.

  • One list, every balance, never out of date.
  • Register a payment against a debt in two taps.
  • SMS reminders, sent when you decide, not by themselves.

FAQ

Questions, answered plainly.

Do I need a card reader or any extra device?

No. ItriPay runs on an Android phone with NFC. There is nothing else to buy or rent.

How much does it cost?

49 MAD a month, everything included. It is free in any 30 days where your revenue stays under 500 MAD.

Can I take payments with it today?

Not yet. ItriPay is pre-launch and we are looking for a licensed payment partner. Join the waitlist and we will write to you when it opens.

Is ItriPay a bank or a payment institution?

No. ItriPay is software. Payments will be processed by a licensed partner.

Which languages does the app speak?

Darija, Tamazight, French and English.

What is the credit carnet?

The notebook of who owes what, kept on the phone: each customer, each debt, each payment, and a total that is always current.
